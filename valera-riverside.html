In one sentence: Valera is a four-year-old building surrounded by newer ones giving six weeks free; when the team cut its own concessions to zero in July, occupancy dropped five points in ten weeks, and the fee, billback and collections basics that should carry a new property are not being charged or filed. Staffing is not the problem here. Pricing and discipline are.

Underwriting assumed $86K a year in concessions. The run-rate since January is $424K.
Where it went: 667 separate concession postings in seven months, handed out at the desk. Three residents received $91K between them. One resident got $44K.
Part of it was accounting. Recurring concessions were loaded wrong at takeover, reversed in May (+$112K), then hit again in June (−$99K). The AM note from Aug 6 says the reversal is done and no payment plans are left from it.

Why
The RPM stopped all desk-level concessions in July. Every concession now needs written RPM approval. That fixed the giveaway. It also removed Valera's only answer to six weeks free next door, and occupancy started falling the same month.
The website still advertised "6 weeks free on one and two bedrooms" on Sep 21. The pricing system shows zero. The RPM says none are offered. A prospect who read six weeks on the website and heard "no" at the desk is a cancelled application. That may be a large part of the 56% cancel rate.

Where the $151K gap to underwriting comes from ($K per year)

Revenue is $500K short: concessions $338K, other income $205K, loss to lease $152K. Expenses are $349K under, and half of that is payroll. Leasing salaries ran at half of budget, the porter line is empty, and $13K went to temp agencies instead. The savings are open seats and a lean first year, not a cheaper building. Once the team is full, the revenue gap is the real number.
