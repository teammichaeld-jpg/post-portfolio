In one sentence: Canyon Villa runs about $500K a year under plan because the rules that earn its property-tax break only let in renters who earn between roughly $72K and $93K, so two of every three applicants are turned away and the one-bedrooms sit 84% full; a quarter of the residents are behind on rent; and the tax break itself has been claimed on 67% of the homes, not the 95% the deal was priced on, which is another $280K a year of cash the model never counted.

The income box is too small to fill the buildingcritical
−$340K

The rule: every home is restricted to households earning no more than 80% of area income. In San Diego that is $92,700 for one person and $105,950 for two. Post also requires income of 2.5 times the rent.
The box: a one-bedroom asks $2,416, so a single renter must earn at least $72,500 and no more than $92,700. That is a $20K window. For a two-bedroom at $2,653 a couple needs $79,600 to $105,950.
What it does to leasing: in the three months to May 24, 612 inquiries became 82 tours, 29 applications, 18 denials and 15 leases, and 18 applicants cancelled. Net leases for the quarter: minus three. Today: 33 inquiries a week, 4 tours, 1 net lease.
Where it bites: one-bedrooms were 84% occupied on June 1 (7 of 43 empty) against 93% for two-bedrooms. 18 homes are empty today, 7 ready to show, 11 not. Occupancy has sat between 88% and 92% all year.

Why it matters
Every point of occupancy is about $59K a year. The gap to budget is five points, about $300K, and the deal was priced at 97.5%.
The business plan said "stabilize at 95%" and never asked who can actually qualify. The fix is to widen the box from both sides: lower the one-bedroom asking rent so the floor drops, and let an income-qualified applicant in at 2.25 times rent with a bigger deposit. Both are Post decisions, not ConAm's.
What the asset manager says (Aug 17 email to Kevin Conde): the top denial reason is credit, not income. ConAm's floor is a 550 score; Post's other manager, Aperto, has no official floor and screens on collections instead. Canyon Villa's cancel-or-deny rate was 90%+ against 50% or less at most California properties. Both gates need loosening: credit and the income box. The AM's own compliance count was 49% in January and 59% in August, against a 70% target for the February 2027 filing; the Feb 2026 claim as filed shows 67%.
