The seller's free-rent leases are expiring and residents are leavingcritical

occupancy in 60 days

Bought at 95.1% occupied in March. Peaked at 95.5% in mid-August. Today 92.7%. Projected 87.3% in 30 days and 84.5% in 60.
Why the drop: the seller leased up a brand-new building in 2025 with one to two months free. Those leases are coming due. Post's policy is no concessions, so the renewal offer is the full rent. In August, 13 renewals went out at an average of 15% more. One week in September, six renewals at 39% more.
What residents did: 24 gave notice and their homes are not re-rented. 17 move-outs are scheduled in the next 30 days. 9 left last week. 25 more leases expire in the next 75 days.
The math: about 35 move-outs over the next two months against roughly 8 new leases takes Oslo from 92.7% occupied to the mid-80s, which is what the system's own 60-day projection of 84.5% says.

Empty homes take 46 days to turn on a 2024 buildingcritical
2 of 21
vacant homes ready
›
36 homes turned in the last 30 days. That is one in eight, on a building where a turn should be a touch-up and a clean.
Turn time 46 days. The standard in the business plan is 7. Contract painting is $52K a year on a building that was painted in 2024. The plan said painting would be in-house.
Twice the portfolio. Across the 86 Post properties that report a turn time, the median is 23 days and the average 26. Oslo is the sixth slowest of all of them, on the newest building Post owns. The same regional team turns Valera in 13 days, Mariposa in 24 and Crestmark in 26. Avenues does not report one at all.
Fewest ready homes in the group. 2 of 21 vacant homes are showable at Oslo (10%). Valera has 35 of 45 ready (78%), Crestmark 29 of 42 (69%).
The leasing office is holding units too. The Oct 6 tracker shows homes held a month for move-ins that have not happened, an applicant who missed move-in with no screening decision, and a lease that was never generated.
Why it matters
Leasing can only sell what is ready. Two ready homes against 27 prospects a week means the tours end in "we'll call you."
Same pattern as Avenues, with the same regional team: the turn vendor, Anvic, does every paint; nobody checks the invoice against the move-out; the in-house switch never happened.
Valera proves the team can turn a home in 13 days when it chooses to. Oslo at 46 is a choice, not a capability problem.

Property tax is still on the books and compliance is at 29%critical
$484K
tax run-rate · UW $224K
›
The deal assumed a housing-authority tax exemption. The business plan marks it "done." The books still accrue about $30K a month of property tax, plus an $82K catch-up in August. Underwriting allowed $224K a year. The run-rate is $484K.
Compliance: 58 homes need residents at or under 100% of area income and 230 at or under 120%. Designated so far: 55 and 28. Files completed: 44 of 288. The operating agreement gives 12 months from closing, March 2, 2027. The dashboard shows 2028 and says "On track."
Why it matters
Most residents in a $1,800 apartment clear the 120% test on paper. It is paperwork, and only 44 files are done in seven months. The pace needed is about 40 a month.
If the exemption is in force, the tax accrual is wrong and NOI below the line is understated by $260K. If it is not, the deal is missing its biggest assumption. Either way someone needs to confirm with the Fairburn Housing Authority and Fulton County this month.
