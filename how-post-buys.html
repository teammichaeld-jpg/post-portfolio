In one sentence: Post buys ordinary market-rate apartment communities, puts a housing authority or nonprofit into the ownership, restricts rents to 80% of area median income, and in exchange the property tax goes to zero. Because the allowed rent is usually higher than what the building already charges, nothing is given up and the income jumps on day one. They call it an affordable conversion. Everything else, the markets, the sizes, the brokers, the partners, follows from that one trade.

The engine

1 · Find
A B or C-plus workforce property
150 to 700 units, 1970s to 2020s, in a high-income suburb where rents sit below the 80% AMI ceiling.
→
2 · Structure
Bring in a public partner
Housing authority, tribal authority or 501c3 takes a sliver of ownership or title. A recorded agreement restricts rents and incomes.
→
3 · Collect
Tax goes to zero
Expenses drop 15% to 25%. The 5.5% cap becomes a 6.5% to 7.5% yield. Agency lenders give more leverage. Refinance in 2 to 4 years and return capital.

Why it works: the pullback test

The restricted rent is 30% of the income limit, divided by twelve. In a high-income county that number is often above market rent. When it is, Post restricts the property on paper, changes nothing a resident can feel, and stops paying property tax. When the restricted rent is below market, the gap is called "pullback," and it eats the tax savings. The whole underwriting comes down to that comparison.
Atlanta MSA, 2026 · 4-person 80% AMI limit $94,240
1BR max rent = 30% × 75% × $94,240 ÷ 12 = $1,767
2BR max rent = 30% × 90% × $94,240 ÷ 12 = $2,120
Douglasville 2BR asking today ≈ $1,450 to $1,600 → no pullback → full exemption, no rent given up
That is why Post owns 1,229 units within two miles of each other in Lithia Springs and Douglasville, and why a luxury tower in Buckhead or a cheap property in a low-income county both fail. The deal is not about the building. It is about where the building sits relative to the income limit.

Where they buy, and the tool in each state

Ordered by how proven the structure is. The partner, the restriction and the fee are what make a state workable.

Georgia 7+ deals since 2024

hottest
Partner: county housing authority holds 0.01%. Douglas, Cobb, Gwinnett, Fulton, Fairburn so far.
Restriction: 50% of units at 80% AMI, 50% at 140% (Fulton 120%). Cobb and Gwinnett: 20% at 60%, 30% at 80%, 50% at 120%.
Catch: apply by April 1 or lose the year's exemption. 1.5% exit fee. 24 months to reach compliance.

Washington 13 deals

strongest structure
Partner: Spokane Indian Housing Authority takes title, 99-year lease back.
Restriction: 100% at 80% AMI, flexible to 120 to 140%.
Why it is best: exemption is by right the day of closing, plus no seller excise tax, so Post can pay 97% of asking and still win.

Texas 72 deals, the original

harder since 2023
Partner: housing finance corporations and housing authorities (Pleasanton, CCHFC, La Villa, Houston, Denton, Corpus Christi).
Since the 2023 law: new deals use HFCs, CHDO nonprofits, HUD loan assumptions and 501c3 bonds. CHDO excludes Harris, Tarrant and Dallas counties.
Side business: Post sells its exemption know-how to other owners for $200K to $750K a deal.

California 41 deals

proven
Partner: CMFA regulatory agreement plus a nonprofit general partner (Integrity, Foothill).
Restriction: target 100% at 80% AMI, 40% minimum. Exemption equals the share of units qualified.
Bonus: exempt from state rent control. Also buys LIHTC deals and GP interests. $600 per unit upfront, $150 per unit a year.

South Carolina 7 deals

proven
Partner: 501c3 nonprofit, $0 property tax.
Restriction: 20% at 50%, 55% at 80%, 25% market. Or 40% at 60%, 35% at 80%, 25% market.
Note: Greenville area median income grew 9.9% this year, the best in the portfolio. No exit fee.

Florida 2 owned, 4 bond deals 2026

growing fast
Partner: same as South Carolina, with a 120% tier instead of market.
How: mostly Fannie and Freddie credit-enhanced 501c3 bonds through Public Finance Authority and Capital Trust Authority.
Where: Jacksonville, Tampa, Clearwater, Gainesville, Boynton Beach.

North Carolina 1 deal

thin
Restriction: 100% at 80% AMI, nonprofit. Cheap fees, no exit fee.
Problem: area median income grew only 0.7% this year, so the pullback math is tighter than elsewhere.

Also live

New Mexico (5 deals, AMI up 10%), Louisiana, Wisconsin (Milwaukee bond deal), Ohio (memo written), Colorado (two Denver pursuits), Oregon, Virginia, Arkansas, Kansas, Oklahoma, Michigan.

Not in bounds

pass
New York, New Jersey, Massachusetts, Illinois, anywhere with rent stabilization and no exemption path. Student, senior-only, hotels, under 100 units unless packaged. Retail is a separate desk.

Equity partners and what each one needs

Institutional: Vistria, PGIM, Raymond James, Freestone, Basis, Silverpeak, Enterprise, Walker & Dunlop equity. These write $15M to $55M checks and want 15% to 20% project IRR, 5 to 10 year holds.
High-net-worth and 1031: Conde, Hawkwood, the firm's own investor base (1,600 investors). Smaller deals, faster decisions.
Bonds: Jefferies underwrites; Foothill or Bedford as the 501c3 owner; Post is project administrator and guarantor. Almost no equity, very high IRR, heavier reserves.

Model rules worth knowing: use next year's projected AMI rents, zero rent growth in year one, cut year-two AMI growth in half, 2.5% management fee plus 1% incentive to TAM, insurance growth tied to expense growth. The underwriting is deliberately conservative on rent and aggressive on the structure.

What this means for a new hire who wants to bring deals

Hunt where the test passes. Older garden product in high-income suburbs of Atlanta, Seattle, Dallas, Houston, San Antonio, the Bay Area, Orange County, Greenville, Tampa, Jacksonville. Not downtown towers. Not rural.
Hunt in counties where Post already has a partner. Douglas, Cobb, Gwinnett, Fulton in Georgia. King, Pierce, Snohomish, Kitsap in Washington. Greenville, Richland, Charleston in South Carolina. Harris, Dallas, Bexar, Nueces, Denton in Texas. Those deals close faster because the paperwork already exists.
Lead with the pullback math, not the building. A one-line note that says "2BR market $1,500, 80% AMI max $2,120, no pullback, Douglas County HA" is worth more to this team than a glossy brochure.
Watch motivated sellers. Lender REO, loan maturities, fund wind-downs, year-15 tax-credit deals. Post's edge is paying full price on a deal everyone else sees as fully priced. A motivated seller turns a win into a steal.
Respect the calendar. Pipeline is Monday 1:30 Pacific. A deal that lands in an associate's inbox Monday morning with the county, the unit count, the vintage, the offers-due date and the pullback read is a deal they can talk about that afternoon.
