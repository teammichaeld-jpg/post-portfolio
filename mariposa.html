In one sentence: Mariposa has made about $1.65M a year for three years against $2.44M underwritten, and it is getting worse: occupancy has fallen eight points since June, one rent dollar in thirteen is never collected, expenses run 56% over the underwriting, and the building needs a $914K capital call the property cannot fund. The neighbors are 94% to 100% full at the same rents. This is not the market. It is the building, the collections, and the costs.

Why it matters
The loan is cheap, 2.94% fixed to January 2031, but it started amortizing in January 2025, so debt service jumped from $744K of interest to $1.28M a year. At today's NOI the property covers the loan and nothing else.

Monthly NOI vs underwriting

Underwriting needs $203K a month. The property has hit it zero times in twelve months. June through August averaged $173K, the best stretch of the year, and that was before the occupancy drop reached the rent roll.

Where the $779K gap to underwriting comes from ($K per year)

Rents are actually $514K above underwriting, because tax-credit limits rose and 51 vouchers pay at the housing authority rate. All of that and more is given back through vacancy, bad debt and expenses. The expense side is the bigger half: $580K of the $779K. One caution on reading the bars: underwriting had no turnover line and put turns inside repairs, so read R&M and Turnover together, $65K over, not $106K.
